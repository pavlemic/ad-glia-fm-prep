# Reasoning about the postdoc's concerns, and about validation with no wet lab

This addresses competency-spec items 5 and 6 directly, using the actual run results (`CROSS_FM_SYNTHESIS.md`) rather than restating the design intentions from before anything was run. Where a concern was only partially addressed, or where the results actually confirm the worry rather than retiring it, that is stated plainly — a design decision that *responds to* a concern is not the same as evidence that the concern turned out to be unfounded.

---

## Concern 1: "predictions deliberately suit a particular view"

**Design response:** CPT (masked-gene reconstruction) instead of supervised fine-tuning. No external label is ever injected — there is no axis (AD-vs-control, APOE genotype, cell state) the training loss is told to organize the embedding around. This directly removes the mechanism the concern names: a supervised head can only be as unbiased as the label and the loss you chose to optimize it against, and CPT has neither.

**What the results actually show:** this response is real but incomplete. The training signal being label-free doesn't make the *pipeline* view-free — every layer upstream of CPT is a human choice that shapes what the model can possibly recover: which genes made the vocab intersection, which HVGs scVI selected, which cluster labels became "substate," which two evals were pre-registered as the things worth looking for. Detector #1 shows CPT moves the embedding by a real, non-trivial amount in both FMs (§2 of the synthesis) — but neither eval that was built to check *what* moved (substate structure, APOE recovery) sees it. That is the honest version of this concern surviving contact with data: the absence of an imposed axis didn't manifest as "then the natural axes emerged instead" — it manifested as "something moved, and none of the axes we thought to look for is what moved." **This is not a refutation of the concern; it is evidence the concern was worth having** — an unsupervised training signal is only as interpretable as the evals built around it, and here the evals came up empty against a real effect.

## Concern 2: "don't know what vector space it is"

**Design response:** none, directly — this concern isn't something a design choice retires, only something an evaluation battery can characterize.

**What the results actually show:** this concern is the best-supported of the three. Geneformer and scGPT, given the identical 142,588-cell substrate, produce embeddings that don't even agree with each other zero-shot — lineage-separation silhouette 0.208 vs 0.438, study-batch silhouette 0.044 vs −0.002 (§1). Two "foundation models" of the same data are not comparable representations by default; treating either one's vector space as *a* single-cell representation space, rather than *an* architecture-specific one, would already be a mistake the postdoc's framing was warning against. CPT then moves each space by a different, architecture-dependent amount (16x apart in substate-relative terms, §2) in a direction neither pre-registered probe can characterize. The most honest summary: after this entire project, the answer to "what vector space is it" is still **"we don't fully know — we know two things it apparently isn't (a substate-structured space, an APOE-structured space) in either FM, and one thing it might be doing that we didn't anticipate checking for (something the forgetting probe picks up, §4)."** That is a substantive answer — it rules things out — but it is not a characterization of the space, and shouldn't be presented as one.

## Concern 3: "strength from a specific niche"

**Design response:** the niche (astrocyte + microglia, APOE-stratified) was locked early specifically because it maps onto axes the Stanton lab already works from (APOE4, non-neuronal cells, astro-microglia crosstalk), and the project was scoped down from a broader design (all 3 lineages, more studies, a vascular niche) to keep the niche well-powered rather than thin.

**What the results actually show:** the niche choice bought feasibility, not a positive result. Every check tied to niche adequacy passed (vocab coverage for APOE/TREM2/MS4A6A/CLU/GFAP/AQP4/AIF1/CSF1R in both FMs; per-stratum donor counts above the ≥3 floor) — so the niche was never the bottleneck. But the concern as stated ("strength *from* the niche") implies the scoping should produce a sharper, more decisive result than a broader design would have. That did not happen: eval#2 (the niche's own Stanton-core APOE-recovery axis) is null in both FMs, both regimes, at every extraction point tried, and even scGPT's k-NN sits at-or-below chance (§3). **The niche gave the project a well-powered, defensible substrate; it did not, on the evidence collected, give the project a positive finding the niche can claim credit for.** If asked directly, the honest position is: the niche choice was sound methodology (it kept the comparison interpretable and adequately powered), but "strength from a specific niche" as a claim about *results* is not supported yet — the results are uniformly null on exactly the axis the niche was chosen to make legible.

## Item 6: reasoning about validation with no wet lab

There is no experimental ground truth anywhere in this project — no perturbation data, no functional assay confirming that a cell called "activated" or "DAA/reactive" is actually in that functional state, no independent confirmation that an embedding shift corresponds to a real biological reprogramming rather than a training artifact. The substitutes actually used, and what each one does and doesn't cover:

- **Pre-registration** (`EVALUATION_CONTRACT.md`, written before any CPT result existed) — substitutes for the wet-lab discipline of a pre-registered hypothesis. It prevents post-hoc threshold selection, not overfitting to the substrate itself.
- **Cross-architecture replication** — running the identical eval battery on two independently-trained foundation models is the closest analog to experimental replication this project has. Where both FMs agree (eval#1/eval#2 null everywhere, §6) that convergence is real evidence, not just consistency — two architectures with different embedding geometries landing on the same null is harder to explain away as an artifact of one model's quirks. Where they disagree (the forgetting-probe sign flip, §4), that too is informative: it shows the result is architecture-specific rather than a property of the data or the niche, which narrows where an explanation should be sought.
- **Measured null distributions** (base-vs-base repeat-embedding passes, used throughout the scGPT arc) — the internal-control substitute for a negative control: it answers "how much does this metric move with zero true effect" using the same pipeline, same data, same code path. This is stronger than the fixed pre-registered thresholds alone, because it's calibrated to this specific stochastic embedding process rather than assumed.
- **Donor-held-out evaluation** — the substitute for out-of-sample validation: every eval score is computed on donors never seen during CPT, so a result can't be a memorized training-set artifact, though it says nothing about whether the *labels themselves* (substate, APOE-carrier) are correct.
- **The forgetting probe on Kang 2018 PBMC** — the one piece of this project that touches externally-annotated ground truth (real, independently-labeled cell types from an unrelated dataset). It is not wet-lab validation of the AD/glia biology, but it is a genuine check that CPT didn't degrade the model's grip on real, annotated structure elsewhere — and it is the one place a large, well-powered, non-null effect actually showed up (§4).

**What none of this validates, and what is explicitly out of reach without wet-lab or at least orthogonal molecular data:** whether the substate labels (activated/homeostatic, reactive/resting) correspond to real functional states rather than transcriptional clusters that happen to separate; whether APOE genotype's causal relationship to microglia/astrocyte state is being tested at all, versus just genotype's correlation with an embedding position; and whether the real, measured embedding drift from CPT (detector #1) represents anything biologically meaningful or is fully explained by a training artifact (e.g., the sequence-length confound found in §5, which turned out to explain most of the per-study checkpoint divergence in both FMs). The project's overall epistemic position is: **strong evidence for what the embeddings do NOT contain (recoverable substate/APOE structure post-CPT), weaker evidence for what they DO contain (a real but uncharacterized shift), and no evidence bearing on biological ground truth at all** — which is the honest ceiling of what a no-wet-lab, CPT-based prep project can establish.
